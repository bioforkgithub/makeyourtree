## 1. Who holds the copyright

Copyright in the source code vests in **Manish Prakash Victor** as the author. MakeYourTree was written
without an employer, a commissioning client or a co-author, so there is no question of
work-for-hire, no assignment to reconcile and no third party with a competing claim. The
copyright notice in `LICENSE.md` and in `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md` names the author accordingly.

Publishing under MIT is a **licence, not a transfer**. The author keeps the copyright and
grants everyone else broad permission to use the work. That is what makes it possible to
relicense later, to dual-licence, or to grant a specific party different terms — options
that giving the copyright away would foreclose.

## 2. Contributions from others

There are none as of 2026-09-04. If external contributions are accepted later, the
straightforward arrangement for a project this size is **inbound = outbound**: a
contributor licenses their patch under MIT, the same licence the project goes out under,
and retains their own copyright. State that in a `CONTRIBUTING.md` before merging anything
from outside, so the position is on the record rather than assumed.

Note that this leaves copyright distributed across contributors rather than consolidated.
That is normal and workable, but it does mean a future relicensing would need every
contributor's agreement.

## 3. AI assistance

Parts of MakeYourTree were written with assistance from a large language model, under the
author's direction and review. This is disclosed in the README and recorded here because
the authorship position should be discoverable from the repository itself, and it will
accompany any publication describing the software.

The position taken is the mainstream one: an AI system is **not an author** and holds no
copyright. Under the Indian Copyright Act 1957 the author of a computer-generated work is
"the person who causes the work to be created" (§2(d)(vi)); US and EU practice likewise
require human authorship for protection. The human authorship here — the architecture, the
specifications the code was written against, the review, the testing and the decisions
about what the software should do — is Manish Prakash Victor's, and the copyright follows it.

## 4. Independence

MakeYourTree is an independent, clean-room implementation. It is not affiliated with, endorsed
by, or derived from any other phylogenetics package. The audit trail supporting that claim
is in [`CLEANROOM.md`](CLEANROOM.md) and [`PROVENANCE.md`](PROVENANCE.md), and the
generated-fixture record is in [`FIXTURES.md`](FIXTURES.md).

That record was built when MakeYourTree was intended as a commercial product. It is kept, and
kept accurate, because it now underwrites the originality claim made in the paper — a
different purpose, but one that needs the same evidence.

## 5. History

MakeYourTree was developed from 2026 as a commercial product under an open-core model: an MIT
core with a proprietary desktop application on top. On **2026-09-04** that plan was
abandoned in favour of publishing the entire work as free software under MIT. The
end-user licence agreement, the incorporation and tax planning, and the launch checklist
were removed at that point. Nothing is withheld from this repository.
